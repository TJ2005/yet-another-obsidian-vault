	# **Information Security Policies – Notes**

The cornerstone of any security effort is:

* Implementing proper **security policies**
* **Educating users** about these policies

For policies to be effective, they must be:

* **Flexible** – Should not require frequent rewrites
* **Comprehensive** – Should cover all relevant situations
* **Accessible** – Available to everyone in the organization
* **Readable** – Clear and easy for users to understand

---

# **What Are Information Security Policies?**

* Documented, **high-level management instructions**
* A formal way of stating: **“This is how we do things here.”**
* Provide **general requirement statements** to reduce risk
* Policies operate at a **higher level** than standards or procedures

### Policy Attributes:

* **Mandatory** – Compliance is required
* **Non-compliance** results in disciplinary action
* Focus on **desired results**, not implementation details
* Supported by **standards** and **guidelines**

---

# **Standards**

* A **mandatory action or rule** that supports a policy
* Makes a policy **practical and enforceable**
* Includes **specific specifications** for hardware, software, or behavior

**Example:**
A password policy requires strong passwords.
A standard defines:

* Minimum 12 characters
* Must include symbols, numbers, uppercase letters

---

# **Guidelines**

* General **recommendations** or **best practices**
* Provide a framework for implementing procedures
* **Not mandatory**
* Easier to update than policies and standards

Examples of common guidelines:

* “Use encryption when sharing sensitive files”
* “Drill a pilot hole before hammering a nail to avoid wood splitting”

---

# **Relationship Between Policies, Standards, and Guidelines**

* **Policies** → High-level rules
* **Standards** → Mandatory specifications to support policies
* **Guidelines** → Recommended best practices

---

# **Policy Analogy (Cabinet Construction)**

### **Policy:**

* All boards must be nailed together using company-issued hammers to ensure consistency and worker safety.

### **Standard:**

* Use 11-inch fiberglass hammers
* Use hardened steel nails
* Use automatic hammers for repetitive work lasting more than 1 hour

### **Guideline:**

* To avoid splitting wood, drill a pilot hole before hammering

### **Procedure:**

* Position the nail upright on board
* Strike nail with hammer
* Repeat until flush
* If thumb is hit, refer to “Nail First Aid Procedure”

---

# **Policies Are NOT…**

* They are **not system settings** for firewalls/security devices
* They are **not optional** (unlike guidelines)
* They are **product-independent** (unlike architectures)

---

# **Characteristics of Effective Information Security Policies**

* **Complete** – Cover all major risk areas
* **Organized** – Follow a standard framework (e.g., ISO 27002)
* **Documented** – Have ownership, version history
* **Updated** – Reviewed periodically
* **Communicated** – Understood by all users

---

# **Types of Security Policies (NIST)**

1. **Program Policy** – Creates the organization’s overall security program
2. **Issue-Specific Policies** – Address a specific topic (e.g., Email, Social Media)
3. **System-Specific Policies** – Protect individual systems or technologies

---

# **Program-Level Policies**

* Establish a security program
* Assign responsibility to specific management roles
* State organization-wide objectives
* Establish compliance and enforcement rules

### Components:

#### **1. Purpose**

Objectives may include:

* Improved recovery times
* Reduced downtime
* Regulatory compliance
* Protecting confidentiality, integrity, availability

#### **2. Scope**

Specifies who and what is covered:

* Facilities
* Employees
* Departments
* Technologies
* Processes

#### **3. Responsibilities**

Defines who implements and maintains the policy.

#### **4. Compliance**

Covers:

* Enforcement methods
* Oversight
* Penalties for violation

---

# **Examples of Program-Level Policies**

* Business Continuity Planning (BCP) framework
* Physical security requirements for data centers
* Application development security framework

---

# **Example – Application Development Policy**

### Topics Include:

* Development methodology
* Access to program source library
* Requirement definitions
* Risk assessment
* Installation processes
* Software acquisition rules
* User training
* Change restrictions
* Application testing and logging
* UAT (User Acceptance Testing)
* Post-implementation review
* Protection of test data

---

# **Issue-Specific Security Policies**

* Focus on particular technology areas
* Need frequent updates
* Contain the organization's stance on a specific issue

### Examples:

* Email policy
* Backup policy
* Wireless device policy
* BYOD policy
* Social media policy
* Use of telecommunications policy

### Components:

* **Issue statement** – Defines the issue
* **Organization’s position** – Clear stance
* **Applicability** – Who/what/when it applies
* **Roles & responsibilities**
* **Compliance & penalties**
* **Points of contact** and related guidelines

---

# **Examples of Issue-Specific Policies**

## **1. Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)**

Defines allowed and prohibited usage of company IT resources.

Key questions it answers:

* What activities are allowed?
* What is prohibited?
* Where do users report violations?

---

## **2. Backup Policy**

Ensures availability and integrity.

Answers:

* What data must be backed up?
* Where are backups stored?
* Who can access them?
* How long are they kept?

---

## **3. Confidentiality Policy**

Protects sensitive information in all forms (verbal, print, digital).

Questions:

* What is confidential?
* How should it be handled or released?
* What happens if it is leaked?

---

## **4. Data Retention Policy**

Defines:

* Data categories
* Minimum and maximum retention times (often legally required)

Example:
Financial data for tax purposes must be retained for **7 years**.

---

## **5. Wireless Device Policy**

Covers:

* What devices employees may bring
* Permitted and prohibited activities
* Approval requirements for exceptions

---

# **System-Specific Policies**

* Apply to one system or platform
* Define security objectives and acceptable operation

**Examples:**

* ACL Rules: Who can read or modify data?
* Can users access the system remotely?
* Under what conditions is remote access allowed?
