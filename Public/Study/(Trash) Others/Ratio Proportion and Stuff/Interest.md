# Interest

  ## Interest: Simple and Compound

Interest is the cost of borrowing money or the reward for saving it. It is fundamentally governed by the relationship between the **Principal**, the **Rate**, and **Time**.

---

### 1. The Proportionality of Interest

Regardless of the type of interest, the amount of interest earned ($I$) follows these basic proportionality rules:

* **$I \propto P$ (Directly proportional to Principal):** If you double the amount of money invested, the interest earned will double.
* **$I \propto R$ (Directly proportional to Rate):** A higher interest rate leads to higher interest earnings.
* **$I \propto T$ (Directly proportional to Time):** The longer the money stays in the account, the more interest it accumulates.

---

### 2. Simple Interest (SI)

Simple interest is calculated **only** on the initial amount (Principal) for the entire duration.

**The Formula:**


$$SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$$

Where:

* $P$ = Principal amount
* $R$ = Annual interest rate (in %)
* $T$ = Time (in years)

**Key Characteristic:**
The interest earned remains **constant** every year. If you earn $\$50$ in the first year, you will earn exactly $\$50$ in the second year, and so on.

---

### 3. Compound Interest (CI)

Compound interest is calculated on the initial principal and also on the **accumulated interest** of previous periods. This is often called "interest on interest."

**The Amount Formula:**


$$A = P \left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$$

**The Interest Formula:**


$$CI = A - P$$

Where:

* $A$ = Total amount after time $t$
* $P$ = Principal amount
* $r$ = Annual interest rate (decimal)
* $n$ = Number of times interest is compounded per year
* $t$ = Time in years

**Key Characteristic:**
The interest grows **exponentially** over time. Because the principal increases every period (as interest is added), the interest earned in each subsequent period is greater than the last.

---

### Comparison Summary

| Feature | Simple Interest | Compound Interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Growth** | Linear | Exponential |
| **Principal** | Constant | Changes (increases) every period |
| **Interest Amount** | Same every year | Increases every year |
| **Formula** | $P \cdot R \cdot T$ | $P(1 + r)^t - P$ |

> [!TIP]
> For short periods (e.g., 1 year compounded annually), $SI$ and $CI$ are equal. For any period longer than that, **Compound Interest will always yield more than Simple Interest.**
