## Why attackers target people

- Any employee can be the entry point — the target follows **access + exposure**:

| Employee | Why targeted |
| --- | --- |
| **CFO** | Travels frequently — exposed on untrusted networks |
| **HR Manager** | Holds the **employee database** |
| **Developer** | Uses GitHub — code and tokens |
| **Receptionist** | Receives many visitors — physical/social surface |
| **IT Administrator** | **Privileged access** |

## What is culture?

- An **invisible bond** that ties people together
- The **cumulative deposit** of knowledge, experience, beliefs, values, attitudes, meanings, hierarchies, religion, notions of time, roles, spatial relations, concepts of the universe, material objects — acquired across generations through individual and group striving
- A **way of life**: behaviours, beliefs, values, symbols accepted **generally without thinking**, passed on by communication and imitation between generations

## Why culture is important

- Influences our **beliefs and values**
- Shapes our **identities**
- Gives a **sense of belonging**
- Can **bring people together**
- Helps **preserve history and tradition**
- A tool to **understand human behaviour**
- Promotes **social cohesion and inclusion**
- Factor in **economic development and competitiveness**
- Gives a sense of **purpose and meaning**

## Definition of CSC

- The **knowledge, beliefs, perceptions, attitudes, assumptions, norms and values** of people regarding cybersecurity
- And **how they manifest in behaviour** with information technologies
- Goal: make security an **integral part of an employee's job, habits and conduct** — embedded in day-to-day actions

> [!IMPORTANT] Culture ≠ knowledge
> "Completed training" (A) and "knows phishing is dangerous" (B) are **knowledge**, not culture.
> "Reports a suspicious email" (C) and "stops a colleague sharing credentials" (D) are **culture** — security demonstrated in behaviour.
