## Aim

To study DPDP Act 2023.

## Learning Outcomes

- Explain the need for privacy Act
- Describe rights and duties of data principal
- Describe compliance framework

## Theory

The purpose of the DPDP Act is to "provide for the processing of digital personal data in a manner that recognizes both the right of individuals to protect their personal data and the need to process personal data for lawful purposes".

**Key Definitions:**
- **Data** — Representation of information, facts, concepts, opinions or instructions in a manner suitable for communication, interpretation or processing by humans or by automated means
- **Data Fiduciary** — Person who determines the purpose and means of processing of personal data
- **Data Principal** — Individual to whom the personal data relates
- **Data Processor** — Any person who processes personal data on behalf of a data fiduciary

## Review Questions

### Q1. Explain the need for privacy act in India.

- **Digital economy growth** — India has over 900 million internet users; massive volumes of personal data generated daily through e-commerce, fintech, social media, government services
- **Absence of comprehensive law** — Previous framework (IT Act 2000 Section 43A + SPDI Rules 2011) was sectoral, outdated, and lacked enforcement teeth
- **Supreme Court ruling (2017)** — Justice K.S. Puttaswamy judgment declared privacy a fundamental right under Article 21; mandated legislative framework
- **Global alignment** — GDPR (EU), CCPA (California), LGPD (Brazil) set global benchmarks; India needed equivalent for cross-border data flows and adequacy decisions
- **Data breaches & misuse** — High-profile incidents (Aadhaar leaks, Facebook-Cambridge Analytica, BigBasket, Air India) exposed regulatory gaps
- **Economic necessity** — Data localization and adequacy requirements for IT/ITES exports; foreign investors demand regulatory certainty
- **Citizen empowerment** — Give individuals control over their digital footprint; address power asymmetry between platforms and users

### Q2. Explain the following terms:

#### 2.1 Notice

**Answer:**
- A clear, concise communication from Data Fiduciary to Data Principal at or before data collection
- Must specify: personal data collected, purpose of processing, rights of Data Principal, grievance redressal mechanism, contact details of Data Protection Officer
- Must be in clear, plain language; available in English and languages specified in Eighth Schedule of Constitution
- Required before or at time of collection; for deemed consent scenarios, as soon as reasonably practicable

#### 2.2 Consent

**Answer:**
- Free, informed, specific, unconditional, and unambiguous indication of Data Principal's wishes
- Given by clear affirmative action (opt-in); pre-ticked boxes, silence, or inactivity do not constitute consent
- Must be for specified purpose; granular — separate consent for separate purposes
- Withdrawable at any time; withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent; does not affect lawfulness of prior processing
- Data Fiduciary bears burden of proof that valid consent was obtained

#### 2.3 Deemed Consent

**Answer:**
Situations where consent is presumed without explicit affirmative action (Section 7):
- Voluntary provision of data by Data Principal for specified purpose
- Performance of function under law or court order
- Medical emergency, epidemic, disaster, or breakdown of public order
- Employment purposes (background checks, payroll, benefits)
- Public interest (journalism, research, statistics) with safeguards
- Fair and reasonable purposes (fraud prevention, network security, credit scoring)
- Not a blanket exemption — purpose limitation and data minimization still apply

#### 2.4 Data Fiduciary

**Answer:**
Any person (individual, company, partnership, association, state) who alone or jointly determines the purpose and means of processing personal data. Key obligations:
- Implement technical and organizational measures for compliance
- Ensure accuracy and completeness of data
- Build security safeguards (encryption, access controls, breach detection)
- Notify Data Protection Authority and affected principals of breaches
- Appoint Data Protection Officer (significant fiduciaries)
- Conduct Data Protection Impact Assessment (high-risk processing)
- Maintain records of processing operations
- Ensure Data Processors comply via contract

#### 2.5 Data Principal

**Answer:**
The individual to whom personal data relates. Includes:
- Child — parents or lawful guardian act on behalf
- Person with disability — lawful guardian acts on behalf
Rights under Act:
- Right to access information about processing
- Right to correction and erasure
- Right to grievance redressal
- Right to nominate representative for exercise of rights after death/incapacity
Duties:
- Not impersonate another person
- Not suppress material information
- Not file false or frivolous complaints

#### 2.6 Data Processor

**Answer:**
Any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary. Must:
- Process only on documented instructions of Data Fiduciary
- Implement same security measures as Fiduciary
- Assist Fiduciary in compliance (breach notification, rights fulfillment, DPIA)
- Delete or return data after processing ends (unless retention required by law)
- Not engage sub-processor without Fiduciary's prior written authorization
- Bound by contract specifying processing scope, duration, nature, purpose, security measures

### Q3. Describe the compliance framework of DPDPA 2023.

- **Lawful basis for processing** — Consent (free, informed, specific, granular, withdrawable) or deemed consent (Section 7)
- **Notice & transparency** — Clear notice at/before collection; purpose, rights, DPO contact, grievance mechanism in plain language
- **Data minimization** — Collect only data necessary for specified purpose; no excessive collection
- **Purpose limitation** — Process only for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes; no repurposing without fresh consent
- **Storage limitation** — Retain only as long as necessary; erase on purpose fulfillment or consent withdrawal
- **Data security** — Technical and organizational measures: encryption, pseudonymization, access controls, breach detection, regular testing
- **Data breach notification** — Notify Data Protection Authority within 72 hours of awareness; notify affected principals if high risk
- **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)** — Mandatory for significant fiduciaries and high-risk processing (large scale, sensitive data, systematic monitoring)
- **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** — Mandatory for significant fiduciaries; independent reporting line; contact point for DPA and principals
- **Data Protection Authority (DPA)** — Regulator with powers: investigate, audit, issue directions, impose penalties (up to ₹250 crore)
- **Significant Data Fiduciary** — Notified by Central Government based on volume, sensitivity, risk; additional obligations (DPO, DPIA, independent audit)
- **Cross-border transfers** — Allowed to countries/territories notified by Central Government; adequacy assessment framework
- **Children's data** — Parental/guardian consent required; no tracking, behavioral monitoring, targeted advertising
- **Grievance redressal** — Fiduciary must resolve within 7 days; escalate to DPA if unsatisfied
- **Penalties** — Up to ₹250 crore per violation; adjudicating officer appointed by DPA; appeal to TDSAT

### Q5. Comparative Analysis Commentary

**Company A (Meta — Facebook/Instagram):**
- **Strengths:** Granular ad controls ("Why am I seeing this?"), Download Your Information tool, bug bounty program, 2FA options, Privacy Checkup tool
- **Gaps:** Fragmented policy across Facebook/Instagram/Messenger; consent bundling for core services; dark patterns in settings (buried opt-outs); US-centric governance (Oversight Board only covers content, not privacy); metadata sharing across family of apps; India-specific DPO/grievance officer details not prominent
- **DPDPA Alignment:** Partial — transparency tools exist but policy language not plain; consent not fully granular; cross-border transfers to US rely on adequacy (uncertain post-Schrems II); children's data handling under scrutiny

**Company B (Google):**
- **Strengths:** "My Activity" dashboard for access/rectification; Takeout for portability (GDPR-grade); Advanced Protection Program for high-risk users; Privacy Sandbox for cookie-less future; Titan security chips; detailed transparency reports
- **Gaps:** Policy fragmented across Search, YouTube, Maps, Assistant, Android; consent bundled for core services; location tracking granularity confusing; voice/audio retention defaults on; India-specific grievance officer contact buried; cross-border transfers to US/Singapore
- **DPDPA Alignment:** Strong on security and portability; weak on consent granularity and data minimization (collects by default); deemed consent scenarios not clearly separated; children's data (Family Link) better than peers

**Company C (WhatsApp):**
- **Strengths:** E2E encryption by default (Signal protocol); minimal data collection for core service (phone, contacts); simple privacy policy; no ads/tracking in app
- **Gaps:** Metadata (IP, device, usage patterns, contacts) shared with Meta per 2021 policy update; no standalone erasure/portability tools; relies on Meta's governance; backup to Google Drive/iCloud unencrypted by default; no India-specific DPO; deemed consent for metadata sharing
- **DPDPA Alignment:** Strong on security and minimization for message content; weak on metadata transparency, consent for Meta sharing, independent governance; breach notification tied to Meta's process

## Part B — DPDP Act Summary Document

### 1. Purpose & Scope of the Act

- **Purpose:** Provide comprehensive framework for processing digital personal data recognizing individual privacy rights and lawful processing needs
- **Scope:** Applies to processing of digital personal data within India; also applies extraterritorially if processing relates to offering goods/services to Indian Data Principals or profiling them
- **Exclusions:** Non-digital data; processing for personal/domestic purposes; certain state functions (security, public order)
- **Effective date:** Notified in phases (August 2023 enactment; rules and DPA establishment pending)

---

### 2. Key Definitions (Data, Personal Data, Data Fiduciary, Data Principal, Data Processor, Consent, Deemed Consent)

- **Data:** Representation of information, facts, concepts, opinions, instructions suitable for communication/interpretation/processing by humans or automated means
- **Personal Data:** Any data about an identifiable individual (directly or indirectly)
- **Data Fiduciary:** Determines purpose and means of processing; bears primary compliance burden
- **Data Principal:** Individual to whom data relates; includes children (guardian) and persons with disability (guardian)
- **Data Processor:** Processes on behalf of Fiduciary under contract; same security obligations
- **Consent:** Free, informed, specific, unconditional, unambiguous, withdrawable, granular
- **Deemed Consent:** Presumed consent in 7 scenarios (voluntary provision, legal obligation, medical emergency, employment, public interest, fair/reasonable purposes)

---

### 3. Obligations of Data Fiduciary

- Obtain valid consent or rely on deemed consent; provide notice at/before collection
- Ensure accuracy, completeness, consistency of personal data
- Implement security safeguards (encryption, access controls, breach detection, regular testing)
- Erase data on purpose fulfillment or consent withdrawal; retain only for legal compliance
- Notify DPA (72 hours) and affected principals (high risk) of breaches
- Appoint DPO (significant fiduciaries); publish contact details
- Conduct DPIA for high-risk processing; engage independent auditor (significant fiduciaries)
- Contractual binding of Data Processors; ensure sub-processor compliance
- Enable Data Principal rights (access, correction, erasure, grievance)
- Maintain records of processing operations
- Publish privacy policy in clear, plain language

---

### 4. Rights of Data Principal

- **Right to access** — Confirmation of processing, details of data, purposes, recipients, retention period
- **Right to correction** — Rectify inaccurate/incomplete data
- **Right to erasure** — Delete data when purpose fulfilled, consent withdrawn, or processing unlawful
- **Right to grievance redressal** — File complaint with Fiduciary (7-day resolution); escalate to DPA
- **Right to nominate** — Designate representative to exercise rights after death/incapacity
- **Right to withdraw consent** — As easy as giving consent; does not affect prior lawful processing

---

### 5. Duties of Data Principal

- Not impersonate another person while exercising rights
- Not suppress material information when providing data
- Not file false or frivolous complaints with Fiduciary or DPA

---

### 6. Data Processor Requirements

- Process only on documented instructions of Data Fiduciary
- Implement equivalent security measures as Fiduciary
- Assist Fiduciary in breach notification, rights fulfillment, DPIA
- Delete or return data after processing ends (unless law requires retention)
- Not engage sub-processor without Fiduciary's prior written authorization
- Bound by contract specifying scope, duration, nature, purpose, security measures
- Make available information for Fiduciary's audits/inspections

---

### 7. Data Protection Authority (DPA) — Powers & Functions

- **Composition:** Chairperson + members (qualifications in law, technology, governance); appointed by Central Government
- **Powers:** Investigate complaints; conduct inquiries/audits; issue directions; impose penalties; call for information; search/seizure
- **Functions:** Monitor compliance; issue codes of practice; accredit auditors; promote awareness; advise government; adjudicate penalties
- **Appeals:** Against DPA orders → Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT) within 60 days

---

### 8. Penalties & Adjudication

- **Maximum penalty:** ₹250 crore per violation (significant fiduciaries); ₹50 crore (others)
- **Specific violations:** Breach of security safeguards (₹250 cr), breach of notice/consent (₹200 cr), breach of children's data (₹200 cr), breach of DPIA/DPO (₹150 cr), breach of breach notification (₹200 cr)
- **Adjudicating Officer:** Appointed by DPA; follows summary procedure; principles of natural justice
- **Voluntary undertaking:** Fiduciary may offer undertaking; DPA may accept in lieu of penalty
- **Compounding:** Certain offenses compoundable with DPA approval

---

### 9. Cross-Border Data Transfer Provisions

- **General rule:** Transfers allowed to countries/territories notified by Central Government
- **Adequacy assessment:** Government evaluates legal framework, enforcement, supervisory authority, international commitments
- **Exceptions:** Transfers necessary for contract performance, legal claims, vital interests, public interest — with safeguards
- **Standard contractual clauses / binding corporate rules** — Recognized as transfer mechanisms (pending rules)
- **Data localization:** No general localization mandate; sector-specific laws (RBI payment data, health data) may apply

---

### 10. Exemptions & Special Provisions

- **State exemptions:** Processing for sovereignty, security, foreign relations, public order, prevention of cognizable offenses — by notification
- **Research/statistics/journalism:** Exempt from certain obligations with safeguards (anonymization, purpose limitation)
- **Startups:** Central Government may notify exemptions for specified period (innovation sandbox)
- **Legal proceedings:** Processing for court/tribunal orders, legal claims
- **Personal/domestic:** Non-automated processing for personal/household activities

---

### 11. Comparison with GDPR / Previous Indian Law (IT Act 2000 / SPDI Rules)

| Aspect | DPDP Act 2023 | GDPR (EU) | IT Act 2000 / SPDI Rules 2011 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Digital personal data; extraterritorial if targeting India | All personal data (digital + non-digital); extraterritorial | Body corporates in India; limited extraterritoriality |
| **Consent** | Free, informed, specific, granular, withdrawable | Freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous | "Reasonable security practices"; deemed consent broad |
| **Data Principal Rights** | Access, correction, erasure, grievance, nomination | Access, rectification, erasure, portability, restriction, objection, automated decision-making | Limited to access, correction; no erasure/portability |
| **Data Protection Officer** | Mandatory for significant fiduciaries | Mandatory for public authorities, large-scale processing | Not mandated |
| **DPIA** | Mandatory for high-risk/significant fiduciaries | Mandatory for high-risk processing | Not mandated |
| **Breach Notification** | 72 hours to DPA; principals if high risk | 72 hours to DPA; principals if high risk | "As soon as practicable" — no fixed timeline |
| **Cross-border Transfers** | Government-notified countries/adequacy | Adequacy decisions, SCCs, BCRs | No explicit framework |
| **Penalties** | Up to ₹250 crore (~€28M) | Up to €20M or 4% global turnover | Up to ₹5 crore (Section 43A) |
| **Children's Data** | Parental consent mandatory; no tracking/ads | Parental consent <16 (member states may lower to 13) | No specific provision |
| **Regulator** | Data Protection Authority (to be established) | National DPAs + EDPB | No dedicated regulator (MeitY/CERT-In) |

---

### 12. Implementation Timeline & Compliance Deadlines

- **August 11, 2023** — Act receives Presidential assent
- **August 2023** — Gazette notification; Act enters statute book
- **Phased implementation** — Central Government to notify effective dates for different provisions
- **DPDP Rules (Draft)** — Released for public consultation (2024); final rules pending
- **Data Protection Authority** — Establishment pending; Chairperson and members to be appointed
- **Significant Data Fiduciary notifications** — Pending; criteria: volume, sensitivity, risk, turnover
- **Compliance window** — Industry expects 6–12 months after rules notification for full compliance
- **Sectoral regulators** (RBI, IRDAI, TRAI, SEBI) — Expected to issue sector-specific guidelines aligning with DPDPA

## Part C — Privacy Policy Comparison (Detailed)

**Selected Organization: Google**

| DPDPA 2023 Requirement | Organization's Policy Approach | Gap Analysis | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lawful basis for processing** | Relies on consent (personalized ads), legitimate interest (fraud prevention, security), contract (service provision) | Legitimate interest used broadly; not always clearly explained to users | Should map each processing activity to specific lawful basis; India-specific lawful basis disclosure needed |
| **Notice & transparency** | "Privacy Policy" + "Your Data" dashboard; available in Hindi and other Indian languages | Policy long (4000+ words); technical jargon; fragmented across services | Create India-specific layered notice; plain language summary; prominent DPO/grievance officer contact |
| **Consent mechanism** | Opt-in for personalized ads; granular controls in "My Activity"; but bundled for core services (Search, Maps) | Core services = consent bundled; no separate consent per purpose; dark patterns in "Accept all" defaults | Implement granular consent per purpose; separate opt-in for each processing activity |
| **Deemed consent scenarios** | Not explicitly addressed in policy; relies on consent/legitimate interest | No distinction between consent and deemed consent scenarios | Add section explaining deemed consent scenarios (employment, legal, emergency) |
| **Data minimization** | Collects search, location, voice, sensor, device data by default; "Auto-delete" options (3/18/36 months) | Defaults = maximum collection; user must act to minimize | Flip defaults: minimal collection by default; opt-in for enhanced features |
| **Purpose limitation** | Uses data across services for ads, personalization, analytics | Cross-service profiling extensive; purpose boundaries unclear | Enforce strict purpose separation; no repurposing without fresh consent |
| **Storage limitation** | Auto-delete options (3/18/36 months); manual deletion via My Activity | No default retention period; data retained until user deletes account | Define default retention periods per data category; auto-erase on purpose fulfillment |
| **Data security measures** | TLS 1.3 everywhere; encryption at rest (AES-256); Titan chips; 2FA; Advanced Protection; bug bounty | Strong technical security; but server-side access by Google employees for support/legal | Add client-side encryption option; zero-knowledge architecture for sensitive data |
| **Data breach notification (72 hrs to DPA)** | GDPR-compliant 72-hour process; India-specific DPA notification untested | No India-specific breach notification SOP; reliance on US process | Develop India-specific breach playbook; designate India breach lead; pre-draft notifications |
| **Individual rights** | "My Activity" (access/rectify); Takeout (portability); account deletion (erasure); no restriction/objection tool | Portability strong; restriction/objection missing; erasure = full account deletion only | Add granular restriction/objection tools; partial erasure without account closure |
| **Data Protection Officer (DPO) appointment** | Global DPO (not India-specific); contact via privacy form | No India-resident DPO; no India-specific grievance officer prominently listed | Appoint India-resident DPO/grievance officer; publish contact in policy and app |
| **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)** | Internal privacy reviews for new features; not published | No DPIA publication; no independent audit for high-risk processing | Conduct DPIAs for India-specific high-risk processing; publish summaries |
| **Cross-border transfer safeguards** | Transfers to US (Standard Contractual Clauses), Singapore, EU; relies on adequacy | Post-Schrems II uncertainty; no India adequacy decision; SCCs under review | Monitor India adequacy assessment; implement supplemental measures (encryption, contractual) |
| **Children's data / parental consent** | Family Link (parental supervision); under-13 accounts with consent; no personalized ads | Better than peers; but age verification weak; teens (13-18) treated as adults | Strengthen age verification; extend protections to 13-18; no behavioral profiling for minors |
| **Data governance framework** | Internal privacy review; Privacy Sandbox (industry initiative); transparency reports | No independent oversight board for privacy; centralized under Alphabet | Establish independent India privacy advisory board; publish India-specific transparency report |

**Overall Assessment:**
Google has the most mature privacy tooling among peers (Takeout, My Activity, Advanced Protection, Auto-delete) and strong technical security. However, for DPDPA 2023 compliance in India, critical gaps remain: no India-resident DPO/grievance officer, consent bundling for core services, defaults favoring maximum collection, no India-specific breach notification SOP, cross-border transfer uncertainty, and lack of independent India oversight. Priority actions: appoint India DPO, flip collection defaults to minimal, implement granular consent per purpose, establish India breach notification SOP, publish India transparency report.
