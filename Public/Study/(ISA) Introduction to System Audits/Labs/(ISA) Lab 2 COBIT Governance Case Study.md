# Experiment No. 2

## Aim

To study corporate governance and governance of enterprise information and technology, compare COBIT 5 with COBIT 2019, and conduct a public-information governance assessment of Amazon, Alphabet (Google), and Infosys.

## Title

Case Study on Corporate Governance and Governance of Enterprise IT and Its Audit

## Scope and Method

The assessment uses the supplied COBIT 5 and COBIT 2019 publications and the latest official annual reports, proxy statements, governance reports, committee charters, ESG reports, and investor-relations pages available on 27 July 2026.

| Rating | Interpretation |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Initial: governance is informal or evidence is absent. |
| 2 | Developing: some repeatable practices exist, but coverage is incomplete. |
| 3 | Defined: responsibilities and processes are documented. |
| 4 | Managed: governance is documented, monitored and supported by strong evidence. |
| 5 | Optimized: integrated governance, measurable oversight, transparent reporting and continuous improvement are evident. |

> [!important]
> This is an external, evidence-based assessment, not a formal COBIT capability audit. A formal audit would require internal policies, minutes, control testing, interviews and operating-effectiveness evidence.

# Part A - Governance Concepts

## IT Governance

IT governance is the system through which an organisation evaluates stakeholder needs, sets direction, assigns decision rights and monitors the use of information and technology. Its purpose is to align I&T with enterprise strategy, realise benefits, optimise risk and resources, and maintain stakeholder transparency.

## Benefits and Challenges

| Benefits | Challenges |
| --- | --- |
| Aligns technology investment with business objectives. | Lack of executive sponsorship or employee understanding. |
| Improves accountability, decisions and transparency. | Difficulty assigning ownership across business and IT. |
| Optimises cost, assets, people and technology. | Rapid technological, threat and regulatory change. |
| Improves compliance, risk management and cybersecurity. | Insufficient governance data and performance measures. |
| Reduces failed projects and uncontrolled changes. | Resistance to standardisation and control. |

## Core Areas

| Area | Governance Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| IT asset management | Maintain ownership, lifecycle, value, cost and risk information for hardware, software, services and data. |
| Configuration management | Maintain trusted configuration records and control configuration changes. |
| Change management | Evaluate, authorise, test, implement and review changes while controlling operational risk. |
| Cybersecurity | Protect information, applications and infrastructure and keep cyber risk within appetite. |
| Data management | Govern data ownership, quality, lifecycle, privacy, security, retention and availability. |
| Disaster recovery | Restore critical services and data within approved recovery objectives. |
| IT governance framework | Define decision rights, policies, processes, structures and accountability. |
| IT service management | Plan, deliver, support and measure services against business requirements. |
| Process improvement | Measure performance, identify gaps and continually improve capability and outcomes. |

## Governance Objectives an Auditor Should Understand

| Objective | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| EDM01 - Ensured Governance Framework Setting and Maintenance | Establish an enterprise-aligned, transparent and compliant governance approach for I&T. |
| EDM02 - Ensured Benefits Delivery | Secure optimal value from I&T-enabled initiatives, services and assets. |
| EDM03 - Ensured Risk Optimization | Keep I&T-related enterprise risk within risk appetite and minimise compliance failure. |
| EDM04 - Ensured Resource Optimization | Meet resource needs optimally and optimise I&T cost, capabilities and readiness. |
| EDM05 - Ensured Stakeholder Engagement | Ensure transparent stakeholder communication, reporting and effective engagement. |

# Part B - COBIT Framework Comparison

## Latest Framework

**COBIT 2019** is the latest main COBIT framework published and maintained by ISACA as of July 2026. It builds on COBIT 5 and contains 40 governance and management objectives grouped under EDM, APO, BAI, DSS and MEA.

## COBIT 5 Versus COBIT 2019

| Point | COBIT 5 | COBIT 2019 | Audit Significance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Overall model | 37 governance and management processes | 40 governance and management objectives | More explicit coverage and a core model designed for tailoring. |
| Principles | Five principles | Six governance-system principles plus three governance-framework principles | Greater emphasis on dynamic, tailored and end-to-end governance. |
| Enablers | Seven enablers | Renamed governance-system components | Assessment extends beyond processes to structures, policies, information, culture, people and technology. |
| Tailoring | Goals cascade and implementation guidance | Eleven design factors and open-ended focus areas | Scope can reflect strategy, risk, threats, sourcing, compliance, size and technology adoption. |
| Goals cascade | 17 enterprise and 17 IT-related goals | 13 enterprise and 13 alignment goals | Simpler connection between stakeholder needs and priority objectives. |
| Performance | COBIT PAM based on ISO/IEC 15504/33000 concepts | COBIT Performance Management aligned with CMMI concepts | Capability and maturity are assessed from 0 to 5 across governance components. |
| New objectives | No standalone APO14 or MEA04 | APO14 Managed Data and MEA04 Managed Assurance | Data governance and assurance receive explicit ownership and evaluation. |

## Comparison by Required Area

| Area | COBIT 5 | COBIT 2019 | Main Improvement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IT asset management | BAI09 Manage Assets | BAI09 Managed Assets | Same lifecycle control, now assessed in a tailored governance system. |
| Configuration management | BAI10 Manage Configuration | BAI10 Managed Configuration | Stronger connection to information flows, DevOps and technology focus areas. |
| Change management | BAI06 and BAI07 | BAI06 Managed IT Changes and BAI07 Managed IT Change Acceptance and Transitioning | Explicit IT-change terminology and Agile/DevOps tailoring. |
| Cybersecurity | APO13 and DSS05 | APO13 Managed Security and DSS05 Managed Security Services | Cybersecurity focus area influenced by threat, compliance and sourcing factors. |
| Data management | Distributed across the information enabler and processes | **APO14 Managed Data** | Enterprise data ownership, quality and lifecycle governance become explicit. |
| Disaster recovery | DSS04 Manage Continuity | DSS04 Managed Continuity | Prioritised according to enterprise risk and business dependence on I&T. |
| IT governance framework | EDM01 and APO01 | EDM01 and APO01 | Adds governance-framework and governance-system design principles. |
| IT service management | APO09 and DSS01-DSS04 | APO09 and DSS01-DSS04 retained | Tailored using sourcing, implementation and adoption design factors. |
| Process improvement | APO11, MEA01-MEA03 and PAM | APO11, MEA01-MEA04 and CPM | Adds Managed Assurance and evaluates all components. |

## Governance Risks Observed

| Organisation | Key Governance Risks |
| --- | --- |
| Amazon | Founder remains Executive Chair; risk oversight is distributed; complex cloud/logistics/seller supply chain; AI and data-centre resource risks. |
| Alphabet | Dual-class founder control; substantial AI, privacy, competition and content risk; new Risk Committee has limited operating history; AI infrastructure resource demand. |
| Infosys | Global client-access and cyber concentration; responsible-AI/IP obligations; supplier and fourth-party exposure; multi-jurisdiction compliance complexity. |

## Three Corporate Governance Improvements

1. Publish a consolidated dashboard mapping material risks, owners, indicators, committee decisions and remediation.
2. Strengthen independent challenge: Amazon should reconsider an independent Chair, Alphabet should evaluate sunset provisions for super-voting shares, and Infosys should maintain Board refreshment.
3. Expand external assurance over material ESG, workforce, AI-governance and supply-chain indicators.

## Two IT Governance Improvements

1. Publish a COBIT-aligned I&T governance map connecting enterprise goals to objective owners, target capability, risks, benefits and assurance.
2. Maintain an integrated technology portfolio and benefits register covering AI, cloud, cybersecurity, data, technical debt and retirement.

## Two Cybersecurity Governance Improvements

1. Establish a Board-approved cyber-risk appetite dashboard with thresholds for identity, ransomware, cloud concentration, privacy, vendors, AI and recovery.
2. Require independent resilience exercises, red teaming, recovery tests and critical supplier/fourth-party assessments, with material findings reported to the Board.

# Conclusion

Based on public evidence and the COBIT 2019 EDM assessment, **Infosys demonstrates the strongest overall governance practices**, scoring **23/25**, followed by Alphabet at **22/25** and Amazon at **21/25**.

Infosys ranks first because its EDM01 evidence shows a trusteeship model, Chair/CEO separation, 80% Board independence and independent committee oversight. Its EDM03 evidence includes dedicated risk governance, Board cyber oversight and a disclosed cyber/privacy framework. Its EDM05 evidence includes integrated, governance, risk, BRSR and assured ESG reporting supported by Stakeholders Relationship and ESG committees.

Alphabet demonstrates exceptional benefits and resource governance, but founder-controlled super-voting shares reduce shareholder influence. Amazon has strong independence, one-share-one-vote rights and mature committee oversight, but risk and ESG responsibilities are distributed rather than placed under dedicated enterprise risk and ESG committees.

This conclusion is limited to public evidence. A definitive COBIT audit would require internal records and testing to confirm operating effectiveness.
