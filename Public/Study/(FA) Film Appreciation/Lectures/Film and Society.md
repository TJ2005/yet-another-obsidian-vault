# Film and Society

> [!INFO] Core claim
> Films are **not made in a vacuum** — understand the context. A film can serve a multitude of purposes: **art for art's sake, or engagement with social systems.**

## Indian cinema confronting society (2015 onwards)

| Film | Issue it engages |
| --- | --- |
| *Manjhi — The Mountain Man* | Caste, determination, land |
| *Fandry* | Caste discrimination (Marathi) |
| *Masaan* | Caste, morality, death |
| *Pink* | Consent, patriarchy, law |
| *Jai Bhim* | Caste, police brutality, justice |
| *Kaala* | Dalit identity, urban land politics |
| *Article 15* | Caste, affirmative action, rural police |
| *Sairat* | Inter-caste love, its brutal end |
| *Thappad* | Domestic violence, respect |

## Influence of cinema on society

- **Purpose of cinema**: entertainment, but also discussion and critique
- Films can change perceptions — which films changed yours?
- Cinema mirrors and shapes ideology, aspirations and norms (see also [[Genre Theory]] — genres "encode ideologies")

> [!question] Think
> Which of the films above is "art for art's sake", which is a "social system" film — or are they the same thing? Where does the boundary blur?
