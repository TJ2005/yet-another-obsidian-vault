## 1. Semiotics of Film

> [!info] Definition
> **Semiotics** — the study of signs and their meanings. Film semiotics is the specific language through which filmmaking borrows from the other arts — see [[Film and Other Art Forms]].

| Term | Definition | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sign** | A unit of meaning formed by the union of signifier and signified | — |
| **Signifier** | The form the sign takes (what you see/hear) | The colour red |
| **Signified** | The concept the sign evokes | Danger, passion |
| **Icon** | A sign resembling its referent | Smoke → fire |
| **Index** | A sign pointing to something else via a causal/direct link — **show, don't tell** | Wet streets → rain |
| **Symbol** | A sign whose meaning rests on arbitrary cultural convention | A flag, a red rose |

- Christian Metz: "Films are hard to explain because they are so easy to understand."

> [!question] Analysis patterns
> What icons does *Haider* use? What does *The Perks of Being a Wallflower* use? Read the film as a system of signs before judging story.

## 2. Vocabulary of Film

### Units of the film

| Unit | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Frame** | A single still image projected on screen — one instant in time |
| **Shot** | A continuous, unbroken sequence of frames from a single camera |
| **Scene** | A series of connected shots forming one episode — usually one location, one event |
| **Sequence** | A series of related scenes forming a distinct narrative unit — broader span, thematic or chronological coherence |

### Camera angles

| Angle | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Low** | Subject powerful, dominant |
| **High** | Subject small, vulnerable |
| **Eye-level** | Neutral, natural |
| **Dutch** | Tilted horizon — unease, disorientation |

![[p19_img_13.png]] ![[p19_img_14.png]] ![[p19_img_15.png]]

| Angle | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Over the shoulder** | Conversation framing, spatial context |
| **Shoulder-level** | Cinematic close framing |
| **Overhead** | God's-eye — scale, isolation, pattern |

> [!warning] 180° rule
> Cameras must stay on one side of the line between two characters; crossing it reverses screen positions and disorients the audience.

### Camera movement

| Movement | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Static | No movement — non-distracting |
| Pan / whip pan | Horizontal rotation; reveal/follow; energy |
| Tilt | Vertical rotation; verticality, awe |
| Push in / Pull out | Emphasise action or isolate a character |
| Roll | Uneasiness, disconcert |
| Zoom | Lens-only move |
| Arc | Camera circles the subject |
| Tracking | Camera physically travels with the subject — extended time; dystopian films |

### Editing

| Technique | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Continuity editing | Seamless, invisible space/time |
| Cross cutting | Alternating lines of action |
| Cutaway | Insert of related detail |
| Dissolve | Shot fades into next — time passage |
| Fade | To/from black — segment bounds |
| J & L cut | Audio leads (J) or lags (L) the picture |
| Montage | Rapid shots compressing time/emotion |
| Jump cut | Abrupt cut in same shot — discontinuity (New Wave signature) |

## 3. Elements of Sound

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Diegetic sound** | Exists inside the film's world — characters can hear it (dialogue, footsteps, on-screen radio) |
| **Non-diegetic sound** | Outside the film's world — only the audience hears it (score, narration) |
| **Soundtrack** | The assembled music of the film, including pre-existing songs |
| **Score** | Music composed for the film |
| **Narration / perspective** | Framing and camera movement tied to a character's emotions; sound translates internal thoughts into external sources; internality through expressions |

> [!note] Function
> Sound builds emotion and complements the scene — and can acquire central importance. Analysis target for Assignment 1.

## 4. Tools of Expression — Mise-en-Scène

> [!info] Definition
> Everything placed in front of the camera: arrangement of actors, scenery and props. Theatre term (early 19th century); popularised for cinema by **André Bazin** — choreographing movement *within* the scene rather than through editing.

### Two modes

| Mode | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Naturalistic | The world matches our own — familiar, realistic |
| Theatrical | Exaggerated worlds — sets, costumes, not meant to be normal |

### Components

| Component | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| Production design | Set production, colour |
| Cinematography | Camera movement, lighting techniques, composition, transitions, editing |
| Blocking | Actor's movement and engagement with the environment |

### The ten elements

Location • Props • Staging • Colour • Sound & music • Camera angle • Lighting • Actor blocking • Framing • Composition

![[p30_img_16.png]]

| Lighting setup | Definition / effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Three-point** (key + fill + backlight) | Standard, flattering, legible |
| **Low-key** | Strong shadows, high contrast — noir, dread |
| **High-key** | Even, bright, shadowless — comedy, optimism |

### The five composition questions

1. How are the actors positioned?
2. How is the scene lit?
3. What are the shooting angles?
4. What are we seeing that the characters may not be?
5. Is there movement — in the scene or of the camera?

### Analysis checklist

| Lens | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| Setting | How does physical space reflect the characters' inner world — class, culture, routine? |
| Lighting & colour | Purpose? Warm vs cool? |
| Costume & props | What do clothing choices and props indicate? |
| Actor movement & framing | What do movement, expressions and framing say? |
| Theme — atmosphere | How is the director's vision communicated? |
