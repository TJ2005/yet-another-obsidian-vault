# Genre Theory

> [!INFO] Definition
> Movie genres are categories defining films by narrative or stylistic elements — similarities between groups of films based on aesthetic, social, institutional, cultural and psychological aspects. Genre helps determine **characters, setting, plot, structure and tone**: a set of conventions.

## Functions of genre

- **Audiences** know what to expect
- **Filmmakers** communicate meaning efficiently
- Genres can be **subverted or blended**
- Shared characteristics shape film production and **marketing** — a framework of expectations

## Origins and evolution

- Genres arise from a **social and historical perspective** — the dominant trends and ideologies of an age
- Genres **evolve**: Formation → Parody → Experimentation

## Steve Neale (1980)

- Cinema **"functions as a social institution"** — shaped by social, economic, political and cultural forces
- Genres are **"specific variations of the interplay of codes"** passing from film to film through **repetition and difference**

| Neale's claims | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Genres encode ideologies | War films, the **male gaze** — built-in worldviews |
| Signifying processes | Genres create "meanings and positions" via codes: costumes, music, dialogue delivery |
| Codes | Established relationships between signs and signifiers — films group through combinations of codes |
| Specific variations | LoTR vs Sabrina the Teenage Witch — same genre web, different position |

## Genre and narrative

- All genres share a narrative skeleton (three-act, see [[Narrative — Three-Act Structure, Characters and Auteur]]) — but **conflicts differ**
- Same themes (crime, love, justice) represented differently in melodrama, crime, comedy
- Classification: how are **disruption and equilibrium** articulated?
- **Technical codes** — camera, editing, movement, lighting, set, costumes — also determine genre
- Realism is a **contrast/paradox of perceptions**: costumes and settings pushed for authenticity, with concessions for dialogue, performance, behaviour — a constant "**mode of authenticity**" across genres
- ...but genres are **promoted and shaped by marketing**
