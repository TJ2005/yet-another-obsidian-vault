# Narrative — Three-Act Structure, Characters and Auteur

## What narrative comprises

| Element | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| **Plot** | The arrangement of events — typically the three-act structure |
| **Character** | Minor/Major, Dynamic/Static, Antagonist/Protagonist, Round/Flat-Stock; responses to conflict, dialogue, character development |
| **Setting** | Main environment, social conditions, time period, location, weather — **backdrop** (passive) or **integral** (setting controls the characters, e.g. New York) |
| **Theme** | Central idea — love, death, betrayal, patriotism |
| **Point of view** | Whose story, whose eyes |

## Three-act structure

![[p41_img_21.png]]

![[p42_img_22.png]]

| Act | Function |
| --- | --- |
| **Act 1 — Setup** | Hook (how the author captures attention), setting (time, location, context), the problem/conflict introduced |
| **Act 2 — Confrontation** | Rising action — the struggle the main character faces; problem deepens |
| **Act 3 — Resolution** | **Climax** (peak of tension), then resolution — how the problem/conflict is solved; conclusion — how the story ends |

> [!note] Story mountain
> Hook → setting → rising action → climax → falling action (thinning out) → resolution → conclusion.

## Characters

Revealed through **dialogue**, **action**, **relationships**, **visuals** (costumes, makeup, hair) and **backstory**.

## Auteur Theory

- The **director is the author** of the film
- Many teams work on a set — but the director imposes a **unified, coherent picture**
- The director's personal influence, individual sensibility and artistic vision is **identifiable across their work**
- Celebrates artistry *at the heart of the studio system*
- Championed by the French New Wave and *Cahiers du Cinéma* (see [[20th Century Film Movements]])

> [!example] Think in auteur terms
> How would you recognise a Nolan, a Scorsese, a Wes Anderson film without a title card? What are their recurring signatures (theme, craft, actors)?
