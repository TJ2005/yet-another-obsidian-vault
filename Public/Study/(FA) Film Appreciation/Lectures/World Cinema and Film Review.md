# World Cinema and Film Review

## World Cinema

- Films produced **outside the dominant system** (i.e. not Hollywood)
- Diverse styles, genres and cultural perspectives from filmmakers around the globe
- **International film festivals — Cannes, Sundance — are crucial for promoting world cinema**
- Challenge traditional narrative structures; often incorporate local languages and storytelling techniques — **new modes of representation**

> [!note] Festivals + censorship
- Film festivals, film society movements and film censorship are Unit 5 topics — this connects directly.

## Film Review Template

1. **Title & Director** — e.g. *Children of Heaven* by Majid Majidi (1997)
2. **Genre** — family drama / social realism / children's film
3. **Story** — 1–2 sentences on the premise
4. **Performances** — child actors, natural and convincing?
5. **Cinematography** — real locations, child's-eye view?
6. **Sound** — natural sound, minimal score?
7. **Symbolism & Themes** — shoes, poverty, family love
8. **Personal Response** — did it move you? Why?
9. **Final Verdict** — worth watching? who should see it?

## Affect Theory

- Highlights the **role of the body** in processing emotion during film viewership — feelings can be as important as narrative understanding
- Interdisciplinary: psychology, philosophy, cultural studies
- Filmmakers can **intentionally design scenes** to evoke specific emotional responses, enhancing connection to the narrative
- Shifts analysis **from meaning alone toward physical sensation** — what the film *does to you*
- Recent work: how social and cultural contexts shape viewers' emotional reactions

> [!question] Apply
> Use affect theory when writing the personal-response section of a review — *describe the physical sensation*, not just the plot.
