# What is Cinema

> [!INFO] One-liner
> Cinema = **recording of movement** — the intersection of art and technology (kinema = movement, graphien = to record; short for the French *cinématographe*).

- Composite art form: making, editing, processing/production, **distribution and presentation**
- A medium combining **visual, auditory, narrative and emotional** elements
- Aliases: movie, picture, feature, talkie, flick
- The definition has kept shifting — a response to social and technological change

## Four senses of "cinema"

| Sense | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Venue | Movie theatres |
| Art | Film as an art form |
| Culture | Cinema as a cultural practice |
| Institution | Cinema as a social institution |

- Cinema **mediates our experience of the world** — "It was just like a movie!" — it is not reality
- It develops a **cinematic language**: syntax, grammar — reiterating, departing, etc.

> [!quote] Christian Metz
> Films are hard to explain because they are so easy to understand.

## Birth of Cinema

- Origins = a culmination of **artistic + performance + technological** developments over a long period
- Pre-cinema: the period before the motion picture, in film-studies glossary terms
- Film (the strip) was the crucial invention: **coated in light-sensitive emulsion**, retains the image after exposure to a short burst of light; the strip is segmented into a series of photographs

## Technological Breakthroughs — timeline

| Year | Invention | People / Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1827 | **Camera Obscura** — projected the image | — |
| 1839 | **Daguerreotype** — printed the image on a copper sheet | Louis-Jacques-Mandé Daguerre |
| 1872 | **Zoopraxoscope** — images in quick succession | Eadweard Muybridge |
| 1890s | **Chronophotographic gun** (moving images); Venus in Motion | Étienne-Jules Marey; Pierre Janssen |
| 1891 | **Kinetoscope** / Vitascope | Thomas Edison |
| 1895 | **Cinématographe** | Lumière brothers |

> [!note] Why the Cinématographe mattered
> It both **captured and projected** — a single portable machine — unlike Edison's peep-hole Kinetoscope.

## The Lumière Cinématographe

- Developed by **Auguste and Louis Lumière** (1895), inspired by Edison's Kinetoscope
- **Dec 1895**: first public screening — *Le Salon Indien*, basement of the Grand Café, Paris; 33 spectators, 10 short films
- **7 July 1896**: first screening in India — **Watson's Hotel, Bombay** (arrival of the train)
- Representatives took the films to New York, London, Bombay, Montreal
- **Actualities** (documentary-style shorts): *Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory*, *Baby's Breakfast* — ~1,500 shot and shown worldwide by 1905

## Why images move: two phenomena

| Phenomenon | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| **Persistence of vision** | Brain retains an image briefly after we stop seeing it — the "after-image" — images in quick succession (24 fps) create the illusion of motion |
| **Phi phenomenon** | Brain perceives *movement between* two or more static images shown in quick sequence (two dots blinking in turn look like one dot moving) — the psychological foundation of motion in cinema and animation |
