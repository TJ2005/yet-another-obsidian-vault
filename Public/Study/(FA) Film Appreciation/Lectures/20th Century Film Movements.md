# 20th Century Film Movements

> [!INFO] Why movements happen
> Any art responds to social and cultural factors. Art is most experimental at moments of **social upheaval** — for the 20th century, the First World War is the pivot: "reason and rationality" themselves were questioned.

![[p36_img_17.png]]

## The avant-garde roots

| Movement | Year | Idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dadaism** | 1916 | Anti-art, absurdity, rejection of reason after the war |
| **Surrealism** | 1924 (André Breton) | The unconscious and the irrational — dream logic |

## German Expressionism (1910s–20s)

- Artistic style born **c. 1910**, popular after WWI — a response to **collective anxiety**: nightmarish imagery
- Reciprocal relationship with social conditions after the war

| Characteristics |
| --- |
| High angles |
| Deep shadows / **chiaroscuro** lighting |
| Extreme camera tilting |
| **Impossible sets** |

- [The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari (1920)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IAtpxqajFak) — the movement's landmark
- [Its influence on later cinema](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ecuQdkBx1ic)
- [Chiaroscuro lighting example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=reaU40elX7c&t=1s)

## Italian Neo-Realism (late 1940s–early 50s)

- Post-WWII Italy; **film studios destroyed** → used available infrastructure
- Gaze at **social issues**: poverty, unemployment, impact of war
- Realistic depictions — no studios, modest budgets, **rawness**
- [Watch: neo-realism](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xPDDZSux3UA)
- → Bicycle Thieves (see [[What is Cinema]])

## Classical Hollywood — the Golden Age (1930s–1960s)

![[p39_img_18.png]] ![[p39_img_19.png]] ![[p39_img_20.png]]

- The **Big 5** studios: MGM, Paramount, Fox, Warner Bros., RKO
- Full vertical integration: production, casting, development, distribution
- Emphasis on **stars, sound, Technicolor**
- The era ends with: TV popularity, **blacklisting**, and **anti-trust legislation** against the Big 5
- [Roman Holiday (1953)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ELb4S8P3q20)

## French New Wave — La Nouvelle Vague (1950s–60s)

- Directly **opposed Hollywood cinema**: the studio system, big budgets, grandiose easy-to-follow narratives
- **André Bazin** and *Cahiers du Cinéma*: films had "lost sincerity" — their ability to capture true human emotion
- Championed the **Auteur Theory** — the director's vision is the most important thing (see [[Narrative — Three-Act Structure, Characters and Auteur]])

| Characteristics |
| --- |
| Deemphasised plot; dialogue often **improvised** |
| **Jump cuts** instead of continuity editing |
| Location shooting |
| Handheld cameras |
| Long takes |
| **Direct sound, available light** — live recordings, lighting rarely adjusted |
| Breaking the **fourth wall** |
