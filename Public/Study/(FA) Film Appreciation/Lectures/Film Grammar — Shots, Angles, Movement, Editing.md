# Film Grammar — Shots, Angles, Movement, Editing

> [!INFO] Vocabulary of film
> The basic units and techniques — the "grammar" every scene is built from.

## Units of film

| Unit | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Frame** | A single still image projected on screen — one instant in time |
| **Shot** | A continuous, unbroken sequence of frames from a single camera, no interruption |
| **Scene** | Series of connected shots forming a complete episode — usually one location, one event/action |
| **Sequence** | Series of related scenes forming a distinct narrative unit — broader span of storytelling, thematic or chronological coherence |

## Camera angles

| Angle | Effect / use |
| --- | --- |
| **Low angle** | Subject looks powerful, dominant |
| **High angle** | Subject looks small, vulnerable |
| **Eye-level** | Neutral, natural, relatable |
| **Dutch angle** | Tilted horizon — unease, disorientation |

![[p19_img_13.png]] ![[p19_img_14.png]] ![[p19_img_15.png]]

| Angle | Effect / use |
| --- | --- |
| **Over the shoulder** | Conversation framing — spatial context, POV attachment |
| **Shoulder level** | Cinematic close framing without the extreme of a low/high angle |
| **Overhead** | God's-eye view — scale, isolation, pattern |

> [!warning] The 180° rule
> Keep the camera on one side of the line between two characters — crossing it reverses their screen positions and disorients the audience. [Watch: 180° rule explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iW0bKUfvH2c)

## Camera movement

| Movement | Effect / use |
| --- | --- |
| **Static** | No movement — non-distracting, deliberate |
| **Pan** | Camera rotates horizontally (tripod/handheld); follows a character or reveals new info; **whip pan** adds energy |
| **Tilt** | Vertical — captures verticality; upward/downward; awe/spectacle |
| **Push in / Pull out** | Emphasise a character's actions or isolate them |
| **Roll** | Uneasiness, disconcert |
| **Zoom** | Lens moves, camera doesn't |
| **Arc shot** | Camera circles the subject |
| **Tracking shot** | Camera physically moves with the subject for an extended time — common in dystopian films |

## Editing (montage)

| Technique | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Continuity editing** | Cut to keep time/space seamless — invisible editing |
| **Cross cutting** | Alternate between two locations/action lines — builds parallel tension |
| **Cutaway** | Insert of related detail, then back |
| **Dissolve** | One shot fades into another — passage of time |
| **Fade** | To/from black (or colour) — beginning/end of a segment |
| **J & L cut** | Audio leads (J) or lags (L) the picture across a cut |
| **Montage** | Rapid sequence of shots compressing time/emotion |
| **Jump cut** | Abrupt cut within the same shot — discontinuity, energy (French New Wave staple) |
