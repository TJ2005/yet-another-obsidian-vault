## **Task 3: Cyber Forensics Process (Video Summary)**

The digital forensic scientific process consists of **three main phases with eight total steps**:

## **Phase 1: Data Collection**

- Obtain search authority and legal permissions
    
- Document chain of custody meticulously
    
- Hash and duplicate all evidence to preserve integrity
    
- Create forensic images of storage media
    

## **Phase 2: Examination and Analysis**

- Validate forensic tools before use
    
- Perform detailed analysis of collected evidence
    
- Reproduce methods and outcomes for assurance
    
- Examine storage media, hardware, operating systems, networks, and applications to locate the point of compromise
    

## **Phase 3: Reporting**

- Draw conclusions based on evidence
    
- Prepare expert testimony and reports
    
- Present findings in court-admissible format
    

The video emphasizes **three distinct branches**: Computer Forensics (disk imaging, virtual drives), Network Forensics (monitoring network traffic), and Mobile Device Forensics (handling memory volatility challenges). Organizations should have policies addressing forensic considerations, evidence preservation, contacting law enforcement, and involving outside specialists when needed.

## **Review Questions**

## **1. Explain digital forensic process in brief**

The digital forensics process typically consists of four core steps: collection, examination, analysis, and reporting. In the collection phase, investigators acquire digital evidence by seizing physical assets and creating forensic images to prevent data loss. The extraction and preservation phase involves creating exact bit-by-bit copies of original data while maintaining chain of custody. During examination and analysis, investigators validate their tools, perform detailed analysis, and reproduce methods for verification. Finally, the reporting phase involves presenting conclusions and expert testimony in a court-admissible format.[bluevoyant+2](https://www.bluevoyant.com/knowledge-center/understanding-digital-forensics-process-techniques-and-tools)

## **2. Explain the different types of digital forensics in brief**

The main types of digital forensics include:[geeksforgeeks+2](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/computer-networks/digital-forensics-in-cyber-security/)

- **Computer Forensics**: Investigation techniques applied to computer devices to extract evidence, including disk imaging and analysis of storage media
    
- **Mobile Device Forensics**: Collection and extraction of digital evidence from smartphones and tablets, addressing unique challenges like memory volatility
    
- **Network Forensics**: Analysis and monitoring of network traffic and data packets transferred over networks to detect intrusions
    
- **Email Forensics**: Tracking cybercrimes through suspicious emails, phishing attempts, and email header analysis
    
- **Database Forensics**: Investigation of database systems and related metadata
    
- **Web Forensics**: Analysis of web-based activities, server logs, and web page data

## **3. Define the term "digital evidence." How is it different from physical evidence?**

Digital evidence is data or information stored on, transmitted through, or received by any electronic device that has value to an investigation. Unlike physical evidence that involves tangible objects directly examined (such as vehicles or bullet casings), digital evidence requires specific tools and expertise for access, analysis, and interpretation. Devices like phones or computers serve as containers for evidence rather than the evidence itself. Digital evidence includes emails, text messages, videos, pictures, and internet search histories, whereas physical evidence consists of directly observable and manipulable tangible objects.[wallstreetmojo+1](https://www.wallstreetmojo.com/digital-evidence/)

## **4. List the main objectives of computer forensics investigations**

The main objectives of computer forensics investigations are:[linkedin+1](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/objectives-computer-forensics-igor-milkovski-ma)

- Recover, analyze, and preserve computer and related materials to present them as admissible evidence in court
    
- Identify the evidence quickly and estimate the potential impact of malicious activity on the victim
    
- Postulate the motive behind the crime and identify the main culprit
    
- Track and prosecute perpetrators in court
    
- Design procedures at suspected crime scenes to ensure digital evidence is not corrupted
    
- Perform data acquisition and duplication, including recovering deleted files and partitions
    
- Produce comprehensive forensic reports documenting the investigation process
    
- Preserve evidence by following proper chain of custody procedures

## **5. Give examples of cybercrimes cases investigated in each of the different forensics areas**

**Computer Forensics Case - Coupang Insider Breach (2025)**: Forensic teams recovered a smashed MacBook Air dumped in a river in a brick-weighted canvas bag. Digital fingerprints and forensics identified a former employee who maintained unauthorized access for 5 months using retained authentication keys. Investigators inventoried the recovered laptop and found the person kept data from about 3,000 accounts.[[collegevidya](https://collegevidya.com/blog/types-of-cyber-crime/)]​

**Network Forensics Case - Post-Operation Sindoor (2025)**: CERT-In used network monitoring to identify over 1.5 million cyberattacks targeting Indian websites . Forensic analysts flagged real-time indicators including malicious IPs, malware hashes, and spoofed domains linked to seven APT groups . Traffic analysis showed attack patterns using phishing, DDoS, and malware injections .

**Mobile Forensics Case - India Cyber Fraud Investigation (2020-2025)**: The Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C) investigated ₹52,000 crore in fraud cases using mobile phone records, call logs, and messaging data. Investigators traced investment frauds, credit card frauds, and sextortion schemes through mobile device evidence.[[youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zLdHMkVbEeo)]​

**Email Forensics Case - DRDO Spear-Phishing Campaign (2025)**: Pakistan-backed actors sent malware-infected PDFs to DRDO researchers via email . Internal SOC tools and threat intelligence teams flagged malicious email headers, weaponized document attachments, and command-and-control domain communications . Data exfiltration attempts were detected and blocked through email forensic analysis .

**Database Forensics Case - Star Health Breach (2025)**: Hacker "xenZen" exfiltrated 7.24 terabytes from Star Health's customer databases containing over 31 million records . Forensic investigators analyzed database access logs, SQL queries, and data extraction patterns to understand how sensitive personal and medical data was copied .

## **6. Identify whether the following artifacts qualify as digital evidence:**

**a. A deleted email recovered from a suspect's computer**: **YES** - This qualifies as digital evidence. Deleted emails can be recovered using specialized forensic tools and represent data stored on an electronic device that has value to an investigation, making them admissible as evidence in court. In the Coupang case, forensic specialists recovered deleted data from the suspect's dumped laptop.[wallstreetmojo+1](https://www.wallstreetmojo.com/digital-evidence/)

**b. GPS data retrieved from a smartphone**: **YES** - This qualifies as digital evidence. GPS location data is information transmitted through and stored on electronic devices (smartphones), making it valuable digital evidence that can establish location and movement patterns. The Ledger breach investigation would involve tracking unauthorized access through IP geolocation data.[wallstreetmojo+1](https://www.wallstreetmojo.com/digital-evidence/)

**c. A printed document with handwritten notes**: **PARTIALLY** - The printed text portion may have originated as digital evidence if it was created electronically, but the handwritten notes are physical evidence. The handwritten notes would be analyzed through traditional forensic methods (handwriting analysis), while the printed content could be traced back to its digital source through metadata, printer forensics, or document analysis.[[forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/larsdaniel/2024/11/17/what-exactly-is-digital-evidence/)]​
