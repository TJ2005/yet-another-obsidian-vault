
## FOSS Open Source Journey – 15 Week Reel Plan

### **Week 1 – Orientation (Mindset + Reality)**

**Reel 1:** What is Open Source *actually*? (No buzzwords)
**Reel 2:** Why students should care (skills, internships, visibility, not just “free code”)

---

### **Week 2 – Myths & Barriers**

**Reel 3:** “I’m not good enough to contribute” – debunked
**Reel 4:** Coding vs non-coding contributions (docs, design, QA, community)

---

### **Week 3 – Prerequisites**

**Reel 5:** Minimum skills needed to start (spoiler: very low)
**Reel 6:** Tools overview: Git, GitHub, issues, PRs (big picture)

---

### **Week 4 – Git & GitHub Basics**

**Reel 7:** What Git is vs what GitHub is
**Reel 8:** Fork, clone, commit, push – mental model (not commands)

---

### **Week 5 – Open Source Culture**

**Reel 9:** How open source communities work (maintainers, contributors, reviewers)
**Reel 10:** Code of Conduct & professional behavior (very important)

---

### **Week 6 – Finding Projects**

**Reel 11:** How to find beginner-friendly projects
**Reel 12:** Reading a repo: README, issues, labels (`good first issue`)

---

### **Week 7 – First Contribution Walkthrough**

**Reel 13:** Picking your *first* issue (what to avoid)
**Reel 14:** Understanding an issue before touching code

---

### **Week 8 – Making the First PR**

**Reel 15:** Branching strategy for beginners
**Reel 16:** Writing a good commit message & PR description

---

### **Week 9 – Reviews & Feedback**

**Reel 17:** What happens after you open a PR
**Reel 18:** Handling review comments without panicking

---

### **Week 10 – Common Mistakes**

**Reel 19:** Beginner mistakes that get PRs ignored
**Reel 20:** Why PRs get rejected (and why that’s normal)

---

### **Week 11 – Consistency & Growth**

**Reel 21:** How to stay consistent without burnout
**Reel 22:** Moving from small fixes to real features

---
### **Week 12 – Open Source Programs**

**Reel 23:** Intro to programs: GSoC, Outreachy, MLH
**Reel 24:** How open source helps internships & resumes

---
### **Week 13 – Portfolio & Visibility**

**Reel 25:** Turning contributions into a portfolio
**Reel 26:** GitHub profile optimization for students

---
### **Week 14 – Advanced Contribution Paths**

**Reel 27:** Becoming a regular contributor / maintainer
**Reel 28:** Non-code leadership roles in FOSS

---
### **Week 15 – Wrap-up & Call to Action**

**Reel 29:** Roadmap recap: From zero to contributor
**Reel 30:** 30-day open source challenge for students

---
## Posting Strategy (Recommended)

* **Duration:** 30–45 seconds per reel
* **Style:** Screen + face OR whiteboard explanations
* **CTA:**

  * “Save this for later”
  * “Follow for Week X”
  * “Comment ‘START’ for resources”

# Other Ideas
---

## 1. FOSS CULTURE (Very Underrated, High Relatability)

This builds identity, not just skills.

### Post Ideas

* “Why open source works without managers”
* “How Linux communities actually make decisions”
* “Why maintainers burn out”
* “Open source ≠ corporate software culture”

### Reels

* “Maintainer vs Contributor mindset”
* “Why ‘free’ software still costs effort”
* “Why toxic PRs kill projects”

These make students feel *part of a movement*, not just a resume grind.

---

## 2. REAL-WORLD TECH STORIES (People Love This)

Storytelling > tutorials.

### Content Ideas

* “How a random OSS library broke half the internet”
* “Why a 1-line bug caused millions in losses”
* “FOSS projects used by Google, Netflix, ISRO”
* “Why most startups depend on OSS”

Short narrative reels perform extremely well.

---

## 3. CAREER BEYOND GSOC (Important)

Not everyone wants GSOC.

### Posts

* “How OSS helps off-campus placements”
* “Why recruiters care about GitHub”
* “OSS vs internships”
* “FOSS for startups & freelancing”

### Reels

* “How OSS got people hired”
* “What recruiters actually check on GitHub”

---

## 4. NON-CODING FOSS CONTRIBUTIONS (Mass Appeal)

This **widens your audience massively**.

### Content Ideas

* Documentation
* UI/UX design
* Translations
* Community moderation
* Event organizing
* Testing & bug reports

Reel example:

> “You don’t need to be a god-tier coder to do OSS”

This converts **non-CS + beginners**.

---

## 5. LINUX + SOFTWARE FREEDOM (Core FOSS DNA)

Don’t ignore this — it differentiates you from coding clubs.

### Posts

* “Why Linux exists”
* “Windows vs Linux (ethical, not fanboy)”
* “What is software freedom?”
* “FOSS licenses explained simply”

Memes + carousels work well here.

---

## 6. TOOLING & WORKFLOWS (Practical, Save-Worthy)

### Post Ideas

* “Tools every dev should know”
* “CLI tools that save time”
* “Terminal tricks”
* “VS Code extensions devs actually use”

Not PRs, but still dev-heavy.

---

## 7. FAILURES, CONFESSIONS & LEARNING

This humanizes your committee.

### Reels

* “Our first OSS workshop failed. Here’s why.”
* “Mistakes we made while learning Git”
* “What we wish we knew in 1st year”

These get **comments**.

---

## 8. EVENTS WITHOUT BORING POSTERS

Instead of posters:

### Better Content

* “What you’ll ACTUALLY learn in this workshop”
* “Who should attend / who shouldn’t”
* “Live prep checklist”
* “Workshop recap with learnings”

---

## 9. INTERACTIVE CONTENT (Boosts Reach)

### Stories & Posts

* Polls: “Linux or Windows?”
* Quizzes: “Guess the license”
* AMA: “Ask about OSS / careers”
* Code screenshot: “What does this do?”

Instagram pushes this hard.

---

## 10. MEMES (But Intelligent Ones)

Not brainrot. Relatable dev humor.

Examples:

* “When the docs are better than the code”
* “Works on my machine”
* “Maintainer hasn’t replied in 14 days”

---

## CONTENT MIX THAT ACTUALLY WORKS

Weekly split:

* 30% Skills & tools
* 20% Career & internships
* 20% FOSS culture & ethics
* 15% Stories & failures
* 10% Memes
* 5% Announcements
